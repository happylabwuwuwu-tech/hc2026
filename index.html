❓ Q&A 準備：20 題

💡 回答原則
  • 回答公式：先直接回答（是／否／數字）→ 一句理由 → 指出備用投影片 → 停。每題 30 秒內。
  • 聽不清楚："Sorry, could you repeat the question?" 或 "Do you mean ...?"
  • 沒分析過的："That's a good question. We did not analyze that, so I can't answer with data. It's a good direction for future work."
  • 被挑戰限制時：先同意（"That's a fair point."），再說「論文的限制第幾點已寫明」。不要辯護成沒問題。
🚫 絕對不要說（論文刻意沒有宣稱）
  • The archived GBTM was corrected, refitted, or validated.
  • Trajectory groups outperform a simple eGFR slope.
  • The model is externally validated or transportable.
  • 0.40 is the clinically optimal threshold.
  • There is no leakage at all. / The model is well calibrated.
A. 高風險：最可能被問、也最容易答錯
❓ Q1. The trajectory groups were fitted on the same patients before your cross-validation. Isn't that leakage?
💡 建議回答:

The groups were assigned from the pre-index window, so no post-index measurement entered the predictors. But you are right that the historical GBTM was fitted once on the whole cohort, outside our folds, and we could not re-audit it, because the fitted object and posteriors were not retained. So our validation is conditional on the archived assignments; it is not end-to-end. Refitting the trajectories inside each training fold is our first next step.

Source: Paper II.C, II.D, IV.D; reviewer response R4
📝 註：不要說「完全沒有洩漏」。上游的 imputation 也一樣無法排除（Slide 7 備忘稿）。
❓ Q2. Would a simple pre-index eGFR slope do just as well as GBTM?
💡 建議回答:

Possibly. We could not test it. Our analytic file has one row per patient without measurement dates, so we cannot compute a pre-index slope. That is why we claim only that the archived groups add information beyond 42 clinical variables, not that GBTM is better than a slope. It is limitation 4, and it is a planned comparison.

Show: Slide 12, Slide 13
Source: Paper IV.A, IV.D
📝 註：這是論文明講「沒有證明」的事。承認，不要辯護。
❓ Q3. If follow-up is only one or two months, multiplying by 12 amplifies noise. Is your 41.8% event rate just measurement noise or regression to the mean?
💡 建議回答:

That is a real limitation. Two checks reduce it, but do not remove it. First, the stricter 20% endpoint gave almost the same result: AUROC 0.778, and the trajectory gain was plus 0.083. Second, performance was similar above and below eGFR 60: 0.785 and 0.770. A fixed 12-month endpoint needs measurement dates, which this extract did not have.

Show: Slide 4, Slide 9, Slide 12 / B5
Source: Paper IV.D; Table IV
📝 註：20% endpoint 事件數 14,412 (36.2%)。
❓ Q4. Why did you choose a 7.5% annual decline?
💡 建議回答:

We followed Nojima et al., 2017. They proposed a one-year decline of at least 7.5% as an early risk signal in type 2 diabetes, and set it below the ROC-optimal 8.4% on purpose, to favor sensitivity. It is a screening signal, not a guideline endpoint, and it is not equivalent to a sustained 30 to 40 percent decline.

Show: Slide 4 (ref [21] in B9)
Source: Paper II.B; reference [21]
📝 註：先講出處，再講「不是指引終點」。
❓ Q5. How did you choose four groups? What were the BIC, entropy, or average posterior probabilities?
💡 建議回答:

We cannot report them. The original fitted object, estimation data, and posterior probabilities were not retained. A source-code audit also found a duplicated lag term and censoring bounds that do not fit negative change values. So we use the four groups only as archived labels, and we do not claim that the trajectory model itself is validated. Corrected refitting with 2- to 6-group selection is planned.

B. 結果解讀
❓ Q6. How can three binary indicators beat 42 clinical variables?
💡 建議回答:

The groups separate risk strongly: primary-event rates were 16.6, 40.7, 71.0 and 81.7 percent from G1 to G4. They summarize how kidney function changed before baseline, which a single snapshot of clinical variables does not capture. In joint block permutation, removing the trajectory block lowered AUROC by 0.183, compared with 0.075 for baseline eGFR.

Show: Slide 11, B2
Source: Table II; Section III.C
❓ Q7. Why does the rapid-decline group have the highest baseline eGFR?
💡 建議回答:

This is a descriptive pattern, and we did not test the mechanism. One hypothesis is glomerular hyperfiltration in early diabetic kidney disease; regression to the mean is another possible explanation. What we can say is that a spline on baseline eGFR did not improve on the linear model, p equals .561, so the trajectory signal is not just a nonlinear baseline-eGFR effect.

Source: Paper III.A, IV.A
📝 註：[INFERENCE] hyperfiltration 論文沒有寫，只能用 "one hypothesis" 的語氣。
❓ Q8. Your AUROC is lower than Liang (0.899) and Lee (0.826). Why?
💡 建議回答:

Those studies used different cohorts, endpoints and validation designs, so the numbers are not directly comparable. Liang et al. predicted 3-year ESRD progression; Lee et al. predicted rapid slope decline in about 6,900 patients. Our contribution is the within-cohort test: same patients, same folds, same algorithm, with and without the trajectory indicators.

Show: Slide 3
Source: Table I; Paper IV.A
📝 註：不要說「我們比較差」或「我們比較好」。
❓ Q9. Is the model well calibrated?
💡 建議回答:

We summarized calibration with the Brier score: 0.184 for the combined model versus 0.209 for clinical-only. We did not report a calibration slope or intercept in this paper. Before any use at another site, the model would need recalibration.

Show: Slide 8, B1
Source: Paper II.D, IV.C
📝 註：不要說 "well calibrated"：論文只報告 Brier score。
❓ Q10. Why is the gain smaller in CKD G5?
💡 建議回答:

These subgroup results are post hoc, computed on the frozen out-of-fold predictions. In G5 the AUROC was 0.724 and the gain over baseline eGFR was 0.095, smaller than in the other stages. We did not investigate the reason, so I would not over-interpret it. The G5 subgroup is also small: 2,113 patients.

C. 機器學習技術
❓ Q11. Why Gradient Boosting? The difference from XGBoost looks tiny.
💡 建議回答:

We evaluated ten classifiers with one frozen specification: 100 trees, learning rate 0.1, depth 3, and no tuning. Gradient Boosting was slightly better than XGBoost, by 0.0025, and Random Forest, by 0.006. These differences are small in practice. The key comparison keeps the algorithm fixed, so the plus 0.078 gain does not depend on the algorithm choice.

Show: B1
Source: Paper II.D, III.B
📝 註：[INFERENCE] 被追問才講：GB 是在同一批 out-of-fold 預測上選出的 best of 10，所以絕對值 0.783 可能略為樂觀；但 +0.078 是同演算法比較，影響小。
❓ Q12. How did you handle missing data?
💡 建議回答:

Implausible laboratory values were set to missing. However, the feature matrix we received had already been imputed upstream, before our cross-validation, and we could not reconstruct that step. So fold-wise imputation was effectively a no-op, and leakage from the upstream imputation cannot be excluded. That is part of limitation 3.

❓ Q13. Why emphasize permutation importance instead of SHAP?
💡 建議回答:

The three trajectory indicators are one-hot and mutually exclusive. Permuting them one at a time creates combinations that never occur, so we permuted them jointly as one block. SHAP is shown per indicator against the stable-low reference group, for direction only. Both methods ranked the trajectory feature first.

Source: Paper IV.C
❓ Q14. Did you include albuminuria?
💡 建議回答:

Yes. UACR and UPCR were among the 42 clinical predictors, together with medications such as SGLT2 inhibitors and ACE inhibitors or ARBs. The excluded albumin field was serum albumin, because its unit and source mapping could not be verified.

❓ Q15. SGLT2 inhibitors and RAAS blockers cause an initial eGFR dip. Could that create false events?
💡 建議回答:

That is possible. Medications entered as yes-or-no indicators, and their timing was simplified, so we could not separate an initial hemodynamic dip from true decline. This matters for a short-term endpoint, and it is one reason a fixed-window, sustained endpoint is needed.

❓ Q16. Is it fast enough to deploy? (HealthCom audience)
💡 建議回答:

Five-fold training took 159.5 seconds, and inference took a median of 0.0024 milliseconds per patient on a 16-CPU workstation, with a peak memory of about 209 MiB. So it is light enough for batch EHR scoring. But technical speed is not clinical readiness.

D. 臨床應用與外部效度
❓ Q17. How would a clinician use this model?
💡 建議回答:

It is not ready for clinical use. If it is validated, one possible use is to flag patients for an earlier repeat eGFR and UACR test, a medication review, or a nephrology referral. At the 0.40 example threshold, sensitivity was 0.699 and specificity 0.711. That threshold was prespecified for exploration and is not a clinical rule; it should be chosen after defining the intervention and its cost.

Show: Slide 10
Source: Paper IV.B
📝 註：[INFERENCE] 「提早複檢／用藥審查／轉介」是可能用途；論文只寫 "change monitoring"。
❓ Q18. Did you validate it externally?
💡 建議回答:

Not yet. This is single-center, internal, patient-level 5-fold cross-validation. The extract did not retain site or calendar-time fields, so we could not even do a temporal holdout. Calendar-time and multicenter validation are the next steps.

Show: Slide 12, Slide 13
Source: Paper IV.D; reviewer response R1–R4
❓ Q19. What about death or dialysis during follow-up?
💡 建議回答:

Patients on maintenance dialysis for more than six months, or with a prior transplant, were excluded at entry. But deaths, dialysis starts, and transplants during follow-up were not available, so we could not model competing risks.

Show: Slide 5
Source: Paper II.A, IV.D
❓ Q20. An earlier version reported near-chance performance for the 20% endpoint. What changed?
💡 建議回答:

A patient-ID audit found that two legacy extracts had features and outcome labels attached to different patients. After realignment, the combined AUROC was 0.778, plus 0.083 over clinical variables. The earlier result was withdrawn, and the change is documented in a formal correction record.
